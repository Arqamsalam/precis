      How Précis works

      Imagine handing a long book to a careful reader with a highlighter and a pair of scissors. They read every sentence, work out which ones carry the story or argument, and cut out the rest, keeping the author's own words. That is what Précis does, entirely inside your browser.

      How it picks sentences

      • Centrality. Every sentence “votes” for the sentences it shares important words with, like web pages linking to each other. Sentences many others point to are the backbone of the text (this is the TextRank method).
      • Key terms. Words that are frequent in this text but rare in general (names, topics) make a sentence more valuable, both for the whole document and for its own chapter.
      • Position and signals. Opening sentences of paragraphs and phrases like “in conclusion” or “the main reason” get a boost. Examples (“for instance…”) get less.
      • Your focus. Words you type in Focus on push matching sentences forward.

      Balanced from start to finish

      Précis walks through the text in stretches and gives each stretch its fair share of the word budget, so the ending is covered as well as the beginning. The coverage bar under the summary shows where the chosen sentences come from.

      Reading smoothly

      A sentence like “He refused.” is meaningless without the one before it, so Précis keeps such pairs together. Stray connectors (“However,”, “Second,”) are dropped when their partner sentence was left out, list fragments are finished with a full stop, and quoted dialogue is never cut in half.

      AI rewrite (optional)

      Choose AI rewrite on this device and Précis first picks the author's key sentences, then a small AI model running on your graphics card turns them into smooth, connected prose, like an editor polishing a researcher's notes. It is told to keep every detail, including mature content, and to add nothing; any part it refuses or garbles falls back to the author's own sentences. The model downloads once (about 1 GB, or 1.8 GB for the better one) and then works offline. It needs WebGPU, which current Chrome and Edge have on most computers.

      Listen

      Natural voices (Heart, Bella, Emma, George and more) are a neural voice model that runs inside Précis. The first time, it downloads once (about 90 MB, or 330 MB for the faster graphics-card version) and then works offline. It speaks English; for other languages, choose one of your browser's voices. Microsoft Edge adds Microsoft's natural voices in many languages, including Urdu, but those are generated online by Microsoft. Use Save as audio to turn the summary into a WAV file you can play anywhere.

      Install as an app

      • Chrome or Edge on a computer: click Install app at the top of this page (or the install icon in the address bar).
      • Android: browser menu ⋮ → Install app or Add to Home screen.
      • iPhone / iPad: Safari's Share button → Add to Home Screen.

      The app keeps working without internet. Voice and AI models you've downloaded stay on your device too.

      Tips

      • After the first summary, move the slider or change any option and the summary updates instantly.
      • Use In context to see the chosen sentences highlighted in the full original.
      • Edit the extracted text before summarizing, for example to delete an index or bibliography.
      • For a PDF, choose a page range to summarize one chapter. Scanned pages are recognised automatically; if a PDF's text looks garbled, use Read all pages with OCR.
      • Use Aa Text above the summary to change its font, size, spacing and width.
      • Press Ctrl + Enter to summarize. Every result is saved in History on this device.

      Privacy

      Files are opened and summarized inside your browser; nothing is uploaded. Things downloaded once and then kept on your device: the app itself, the text-recognition engine (first scanned page), the natural voice model (first time you listen) and the AI model (first AI rewrite). Only Edge's online voices send text anywhere (to Microsoft, to speak it).
